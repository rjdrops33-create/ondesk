Typical monthly pricing by tier and business size

Most AI receptionist pricing falls into three clear bands, and the right one depends less on your industry and more on your call volume and how much automation you want layered on top of basic answering.

  • Entry-level ($25 to $75/month): Basic call answering, voicemail capture, and limited minutes or calls, suited to a solo owner fielding a light, predictable volume of calls.
  • Mid-tier ($100 to $400/month): Higher minute or call caps, calendar integrations, appointment scheduling, and call transcriptions, a fit for a small local shop with moderate daily call traffic.
  • Premium or enterprise ($500+/month): Custom scripting, 24/7 coverage, human fallback for escalations, and deeper integrations with CRM or payment systems, built for a busy service provider that can’t afford to miss after-hours calls.

A solo bookkeeper who takes a handful of calls a day has little reason to pay for premium coverage. A plumbing or HVAC business fielding calls around the clock, including nights and weekends when pipes burst or furnaces fail, usually finds the mid-tier or premium bands pay for themselves through captured bookings alone.

Pricing models: subscription, per-minute, per-call, and hybrid

AI receptionist vendors charge in one of four main ways, and picking the wrong one for your call pattern is the fastest way to overpay.

  1. Flat monthly subscription: You pay a set fee for a defined cap of minutes or calls, with overages billed per minute once you exceed it. This model rewards predictable volume.
  2. Per-minute billing: You pay a set rate for every minute of call time, so your bill moves directly with average call length. Short calls keep costs low; long, detailed calls add up fast.
  3. Per-call billing: You pay a flat rate per call regardless of length, which tends to favor businesses with many brief calls, like quick scheduling confirmations.
  4. Hybrid models: These combine a base subscription with overage minutes, or tiered minute blocks with add-on fees for extras like SMS confirmations or transcriptions.

A simple rule of thumb: multiply your expected monthly call volume by average call length to estimate total minutes, then multiply that by the vendor’s per-minute rate. Say you expect 200 calls a month averaging three minutes each: that’s 600 minutes. At a hypothetical $0.15 per minute, you’d land near $90 for the month before any base fee.

Pro Tip: Ask any vendor to quote your bill at both your current volume and a 25% busier month, so you can see how the pricing model behaves under growth before you sign anything.

What increases or decreases your monthly bill

Several factors push your AI receptionist bill up or down, and knowing which ones you control helps you negotiate a plan that fits your actual usage.

  • Call volume and average call duration drive cost more than any other factor, since most pricing models bill directly against one or the other.
  • Hours of coverage matter too: after-hours and weekend coverage typically costs more than business-hours-only plans, and seasonal peaks can push you into overage territory.
  • Human escalation adds fees when a call gets transferred to a live agent, a feature worth paying for if your calls sometimes involve complex or sensitive issues.
  • Integrations with your CRM, calendar, or payment processor, along with custom scripting work, often carry setup or ongoing fees beyond the base subscription.
  • Add-ons like SMS or MMS confirmations, international calling, call recordings, transcriptions, analytics dashboards, and compliance features all layer onto the base price.
  • Setup fees, onboarding costs, and contract terms, including cancellation penalties, affect your total cost of ownership even when the advertised monthly rate looks low.

Apples-to-apples cost comparison to a full-time human receptionist

A full-time human receptionist is a bigger expense than most owners budget for upfront. Median annual pay for receptionists was $37,230 as of May 2024, or about $17.90 an hour. Once you add employer payroll taxes, benefits, and basic overhead, a realistic monthly business cost lands between roughly $2,900 and $4,100.

An AI receptionist plan priced at a small fraction of the human-cost baseline is typical for basic coverage, which is why even a modest plan can free up meaningful budget elsewhere in the business.

A simple ROI check: if an AI receptionist helps you book just two extra jobs a month at an average ticket of $200 each, that’s $400 in recovered revenue against a subscription that might cost a fraction of that. The payback period, in many cases, is immediate. The tradeoffs aren’t purely financial, though: complex or emotionally sensitive calls still often need a human touch, and vendors should be transparent about what the AI can and can’t handle, a point the FTC’s guidance on AI marketing addresses directly.

Actionable checklist and questions to estimate your bill and select a plan

Before you commit to a plan, walk through a short measurement process so you’re comparing vendor quotes against your real call patterns rather than guesswork.

  1. Measure your baseline: Track your monthly call count, average call length, and peak-hour patterns for at least two to four weeks.
  2. Match the pricing model to your profile: High call volume with short calls usually favors per-call or subscription plans; long, detailed calls often favor careful per-minute math.
  3. List your must-have integrations: Calendar sync, CRM, payment processing, and human escalation paths should all be specified before you request quotes.

When you talk to vendors, ask for exact overage rates, sample call scripts, data retention and privacy practices, uptime guarantees, and the refund or cancellation policy in writing. Treat vague overage terms or sweeping claims that the AI fully “replaces” a human, with no clear escalation path, as a red flag.

Pro Tip: Request pricing for both your current call volume and a 25% busier month from every vendor you’re evaluating, so you can compare plans under the same growth assumptions rather than on list price alone.

When AI receptionists make the most sense

Service businesses with steady call flow and a history of missed-call leakage tend to see the clearest return. Businesses that field complex, high-empathy calls often do better pairing AI with a human escalation path rather than replacing one with the other. Whatever you choose, hold vendors to the same transparency standard the FTC applies to AI marketing claims: clear disclosure, not overstatement.

FAQ

How much should I charge for an AI receptionist?

If you’re evaluating vendor pricing rather than setting your own, expect to pay in line with market bands: $25 to $75 a month for entry-level plans, $100 to $400 for mid-tier, and $500 or more for premium coverage with custom scripting and human fallback. The right band depends on your call volume, hours of coverage, and integration needs.

Is an AI receptionist worth it?

For service businesses with steady, predictable call flow and a pattern of missed calls, the math often favors AI reception, since even basic plans run at a fraction of a full-time receptionist’s monthly cost. Businesses with complex or highly sensitive calls may get better results pairing AI with a live-agent escalation path rather than going fully automated.

How much does AI reception cost?

Monthly costs typically range from $25 to $75 for basic plans, $100 to $400 for mid-tier plans with scheduling and integrations, and $500 or more for premium plans with 24/7 coverage and custom scripting. Pricing models vary between flat subscriptions, per-minute rates, and per-call rates.

How much do virtual receptionists cost?

Virtual receptionist services, whether AI-driven or human-staffed, generally price similarly to AI answering plans at the entry and mid-tier levels, though human-staffed services tend to sit at the higher end of that range. A full-time, in-house human receptionist runs considerably higher once wages and overhead are included, landing around $2,900 to $4,100 a month.
